# Pathway survivability and NEC Article 760

## Levels

NFPA 72 §12.4:

- Level 0: no extra protection.
- Level 1: the building is sprinklered to NFPA 13, and the circuit is in metal raceway or metal armored cable.
- Level 2: 2-hour circuit integrity or fire-resistive cable, a listed cable system or enclosure, or an authority alternative.
- Level 3: sprinklered to NFPA 13, plus one or more of the Level 2 methods. Metal raceway by itself is Level 1, not Level 3.
- Level 4: 1-hour circuit integrity or fire-resistive cable, system, enclosure, or an authority alternative.

2019 has Levels 0 through 3 only. Level 4 is in 2022 and 2025. It is not new in 2025.

The 2022 and 2025 annex notes that protection methods may change inside one building, and that a multi-zone Level 2 or Level 3 circuit can be shorted by a device. The annex recommends Class X or Class N for that circuit. That is advisory.

## Voice evacuation

NFPA 72-2022 and 2025 §24.3.14.4 follow the building's fire-resistance rating. Total evacuation with no relocation may use any Level 0 through 4. Partial evacuation or relocation depends on the rating: 2 hours or more uses Level 2 or 3, or a Class N or Class X Level 1 method; 1 hour up to 2 hours uses Level 4 or that method; under 1 hour uses that method. Under §24.3.14.4.6 the separation is at least one-third of the maximum diagonal of the notification zone (§24.3.14.4.6.3). The §12.3.8 separation does not apply inside that option.

NFPA 72-2019 §24.3.14.4 follows the evacuation strategy, not the building rating. Partial evacuation uses Level 2 or 3, with exceptions that allow Level 1, 2, or 3. Total evacuation uses Level 0 through 3. There is no Level 4.

A tone-only total evacuation on a hyperscale hall is often Level 0 or Level 1 under the 2022 and 2025 total-evacuation permission. Confirm that the project is not partial evacuation before you drop the rating.

Two-hour circuit-integrity cable is supported as its listing says. The annex states it does not have to be attached to a concrete assembly unless the listing requires that attachment. Listed 2-hour systems follow the listing, which may require a concrete assembly.

## NEC Article 760

The fire alarm branch circuit serves no other load. The disconnect is red, identified as the fire alarm circuit, and accessible only to qualified persons. It is not on a ground-fault or arc-fault device (NEC 760.41 for non-power-limited circuits and 760.121 for power-limited circuits). That duty is the same in NEC 2020 and 2023.

Overcurrent for 14 AWG and larger follows conductor ampacity without the 310.14 adjustment. Overcurrent does not exceed 7 A on 18 AWG or 10 A on 16 AWG (NEC 2020 and 2023 §760.43, same text). Other articles can require a different device.

Power-limited cable types moved. NEC 2020 lists them in 760.179 and still has 760.135 for the building installation. NEC 2023 has no 760.135 and points cable types at 722.179. Applications stay in Table 760.154. Cite the NEC edition the project actually adopted. The unamended 2024 I-Code pin is NEC 2023, resolved on its own. See `../fp/edition-pins.md`.

Resistance values for voltage drop stay in the voltage-drop skill. This note does not reprint NEC Chapter 9 Table 8.
