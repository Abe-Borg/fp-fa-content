# Heat detector spacing

Every point on the ceiling has a heat detector within 0.7 times the listed spacing (NFPA 72-2025 §17.6.3.1.1(2)). The 2019 and 2022 lead-in is worded as a choice among requirements. The 0.7 factor is the same duty. Confirm the lead-in before you cite the 2025 sentence on an older job.

At right angles to solid joists, spacing is not more than 50 percent of the listed spacing (§17.6.3.2.1, all three editions). At right angles to beams, spacing is not more than two-thirds of the listed spacing (§17.6.3.3.1.2, all three editions).

NFPA 72-2025 §17.6.3.3.3 adds girders. A girder within 4 in. of the ceiling counts as a beam. A girder more than 4 in. down is not a factor. 2019 and 2022 do not have that girder sentence.

Table 17.6.3.5.1 reduces spacing as the ceiling gets higher. The table is the same in 2019, 2022, and 2025. The last row in the pull is 28 to 30 ft, with a multiplier of 0.34. Do not extend the table past the last row.

A heat detector that is integral to a smoke detector is listed for not less than 50 ft spacing (NFPA 72-2025 §17.6.3.6).

Elevator shunt-trip heat detectors still follow §21.4: lower temperature rating, lower response-time index, within 24 in. of each sprinkler (`epo-and-disconnect.md`).

On a non-smooth ceiling, an FM reading (DS 5-48 §2.2.1.14.6) limits heat spacing to the greater of half the approved linear spacing and the full sprinkler spacing. Smooth-ceiling FM labels in that sheet's Table 2 run from 10 ft by 10 ft through 15, 20, 25, and 30 ft squares to 35 ft by 35 ft. Use the FM grid when the sheet is in force. Do not average it with the NFPA 0.7 factor.

Spot-type smoke spacing, beams, and the airflow table are `detection-layout.md`.
