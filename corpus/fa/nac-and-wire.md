# NAC voltage drop and conductor resistance

Notification-appliance-circuit calculations are part of the documentation package: NFPA 72 §7.2.1(8) in 2019, 2022, and 2025.

Design by the end-line-loaded method or by point-to-point, NFPA 72 §18.3.7.4. The pack checks NFPA 72-2025 §18.3.7.1 through §18.3.7.6: starting voltage (name whether it is the manufacturer's rated start or a measured start), Class and length basis, 75 °C copper, both conductors.

End-line loading multiplies the total appliance current by the resistance of the full circuit. Point-to-point walks the circuit: each segment carries only the current downstream of it, and the drops add. Those are different numbers. Do not report the end-line product as if it were the point-to-point result. `calcs/fa` `nac_vdrop` implements both. A worked check in that library (two 0.2 A appliances, 100 ft and 1,000 ft, 18 AWG stranded, 20.4 V start) is 14.04 V end-line and 16.902 V point-to-point. Those figures demonstrate the method. They are not a hall design.

The end-line and point-to-point methods, the 75 °C basis, and the instruction to count both conductors are NFPA 72-2025 §18.3.7.1 through §18.3.7.6. NFPA 72-2019 has no §18.3.7. NFPA 72-2022 has §18.3.7.1 and §18.3.7.2 only, and it does not state a speaker loss in decibels. On a 2019 or 2022 job those methods stay design practice. Do not label them as that edition's text.

Speaker circuits are a different limit: 1.0 dB electrical line loss, NFPA 72-2025 §18.3.7.2.1(1). On a 2019 or 2022 job that 1.0 dB figure is practice, not that edition's text. Equal taps spread along the route may be hand-checked as one load at about half the route length. When the result is close to 1.0 dB, that lumped check is not the record. The record walks each tap as a constant impedance, nominal voltage squared divided by tap watts, and counts both conductors. This pack does not add that ladder solver. Leave a near-limit result open. Verify with the engineer of record. A campus screen on NEC 2023 ohms, method only: 70.7 V at 14 AWG, 500 ft, 60 W is 0.321 dB and passes; 12 AWG, 900 ft, 120 W is 0.713 dB and passes; 16 AWG on that second run is 1.696 dB and fails. A horn circuit at 14 AWG, 250 ft, 1.6 A ends at 17.89 V and passes an assumed 16.0 V floor; 12 AWG, 420 ft, 2.1 A ends at 16.91 V and passes; 12 AWG, 600 ft, 2.4 A ends at 14.70 V and fails. The recorded 10 AWG rerun of that failed run ends at 16.83 V and passes. Do not guess a gauge that has not been rerun. Module loss comes out of the budget before the cable loss. Amplifier failure is a trouble signal (NFPA 72 §10.19.1, same duty in 2019 and 2022). Compare 25 V and 70.7 V when both are available. `fa-speaker-circuit-loss-check` owns the pass or fail.

## NEC Chapter 9 Table 8

Resistance per foot comes from NEC Chapter 9 Table 8 for the gauge, the stranding, and the temperature. The full table stays in `fa-nec-table-8-wire-sizing`. The campus feeder screen, NEC 2023 only, uncoated stranded copper at 75°C, is 14 AWG 3.14, 12 AWG 1.98, and 10 AWG 1.24 ohm per 1,000 ft. NEC 2026 Table 8 is not held. Do not label those 2023 ohms as 2026 code. On a 2026 job they are an assumed screen and the hold stays. Confirm the edition of NFPA 70 (the unamended 2024 I-Code reference pin is NEC 2023; resolve the project instrument separately) before issuing the calc. Where NEC 2026 is adopted, Article 760.41(B) also says the fire alarm branch circuit is not part of a multiwire branch circuit. The held 2023 text bars ground-fault and arc-fault interrupters and does not state that multiwire bar. CEC Section 32 is NEEDS HOLD. The skill elects the gauge. It does not decide audibility.

Pathway survivability (NFPA 72 Levels 0 through 4, and the 3 ft / 20 ft / Class D choices for an emergency voice system) is `fa-pathway-survivability-election`. It is not a voltage-drop result.
