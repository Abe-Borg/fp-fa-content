# Preaction detection, supervision, and manual release

The sprinkler standard sends electrical detection and release to NFPA 72. NFPA 13-2025 §8.10.2 states that duty. The 2022 counterpart of that sentence was not in the pull. Do not invent it.

Heat detectors used to release water have a temperature rating below the sprinklers in the space (NFPA 13-2025 §8.10.5.1). They are fixed-temperature, electric or pneumatic.

Provide an extra detector for test on each circuit at an accessible location (NFPA 13-2022 §8.3.1.7.1). NFPA 13-2025 §8.10.6.1 says each detection zone. One accessible test detector per zone meets both readings.

NFPA 13 requires a hydraulic, pneumatic, or mechanical manual means that operates the preaction valve independent of the detectors and independent of the sprinklers (NFPA 13-2025 §8.3.1.4; NFPA 13-2022 §8.3.1.2). That is the emergency release on the valve trim. It is not the same device as a manual-release input on the releasing panel.

The emergency handle, or the equivalent hydraulic or mechanical release at the valve, vents the priming chamber and opens the valve without waiting for the panel low-air interlock. Water then enters the sprinkler piping and stays there until a sprinkler opens. A test that pulls that handle will open the valve while supervisory pressure is still up. Do not expect the air in the pipe to hold the valve shut.

A manual-release input on the releasing panel is a substitute for the detection leg. On double-interlock that input still needs loss of supervisory pressure before the solenoid releases water. It does not replace the emergency handle, and the emergency handle does not follow the panel's two-leg program.

Read the opened trim sheet for which device is which. Do not treat them as one switch.

Automatic supervision of the piping and the detectors is required when more than 20 sprinklers are on the system (NFPA 13-2025 §8.3.2.4).

## Count cap and in-rack

Not more than 1,000 automatic sprinklers on one preaction valve (NFPA 13-2022 and 2025 §8.3.2.2). The pull reads that cap as single-interlock and non-interlock only. Double-interlock is sized by water-delivery time, not by that count. Confirm the sentence in the adopted NFPA 13 before you rely on the exemption. Single-interlock and non-interlock are comparison-only. They are never the basis of design.

Where in-rack sprinklers are double-interlock, the ceiling detection operates both the ceiling system and the in-rack system (NFPA 13-2025 §8.10.5.5.2(4)).

## Air supervision by edition

NFPA 72-2019 §17.17.2.2.2 is titled for dry pipe and uses a 10 psi change. NFPA 72-2022 §17.17.2.2.2 covers dry pipe and preaction, still 10 psi unless the manufacturer's instructions allow otherwise. NFPA 72-2025 §17.19.2.2.2 requires both high and low signals and sends the setpoint to NFPA 13 unless the valve instructions say otherwise. The 2025 section states no psi number.

On a 2019 job, applying the dry-pipe 10 psi band to preaction air is a judgment. Say so. Setpoints and the nitrogen supply stay on the fire-protection notes.
