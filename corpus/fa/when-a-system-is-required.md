# When a fire alarm is required

A hyperscale hall is often Group B or Group S-1, sometimes Group F-1. That classification decides whether IBC §907.2 requires a manual or automatic system. The classification itself is an occupancy call. Confirm it. Do not assume Group B from the words data hall.

In a fully sprinklered Group B or Group S building, §907.2 often does not require a manual fire alarm from occupant load alone (IBC §907.2, the §907.2.2 exception, and §907.2.10, where IBC 2021 or 2024 is adopted). Practice still provides a full addressable system because NFPA 75 or 76, the insurer sheet, and the owner standard ask for detection and supervision the occupant-load trigger does not.

Sprinkler waterflow and valve supervision tie to the fire alarm through §903.4 and §907. IBC 2021 uses §903.4.1 and §903.4.2. IBC 2024 numbering for the same duties is one section higher (§903.4.1, §903.4.3). Confirm the adopted print.

Emergency voice communication is an occupancy trigger (assembly, educational, high-rise, and similar). A single-story data hall is rarely on that list. An owner can still require voice. A voluntary voice system still follows NFPA 72 Chapter 18.

Public-mode audibility, where NFPA 72-2022 or 2025 is the enforced edition: at least 15 dB above average ambient or 5 dB above the maximum that lasts 60 seconds or more, whichever is greater (§18.4.4.1), and not over 110 dBA (§18.4.1.2).

## Duct smoke is a separate trigger

IMC 2021 and 2024 §606, where adopted:

- Return air over 2,000 cfm: detector upstream of the filters and the outdoor-air connection (§606.2.1).
- A shared supply or return over 2,000 cfm combined (§606.2.2).
- Return risers serving more than one story and over 15,000 cfm: a detector at each story (§606.2.3).
- Fan shutdown on activation (§606.4).
- Supervisory only, not a fire alarm, where a supervising station is required (§606.4.1).

The IMC trigger is the return. NFPA 90A puts a detector on a supply over 2,000 cfm, downstream of the filters and ahead of the branches, and that detector stops the fan (2021 §6.4.2.1(1) and §6.4.3.1; 2024 §11.4.2.1(1) and §11.4.3.1). Where both apply, provide a detector wherever either rule trips. IBC §907 does not contain the HVAC shutdown rule.

On an FM data-processing room, early-warning detection and a constantly attended alarm path drive the system whether or not §907.2 does. FM DS 5-32 does not want automatic smoke exhaust in that room. If a code forces exhaust, start it from waterflow, not from the early-warning detector. See `duct-smoke.md` on the fire-protection side.
