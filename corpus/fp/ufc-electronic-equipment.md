# Federal electronic-equipment areas

UFC 3-600-01 is a Department of Defense instrument. Density tables, C-factor permission, and the performance-based appendix are `ufc-outside-pin.md`. This note is the electronic-equipment section of that document, Change 6 (2021). On a commercial hall it is a comparison. It is not the basis of design.

Check the service before citing the section. Change 6 tells Air Force facilities to follow a separate mission-continuity guide in lieu of this section. The held Change 1 of that guide (21 April 2021) recasts itself as recommended supplemental features. Army, Navy, and other Defense facilities stay on the UFC section below. A commercial project stays on the adopted IBC and IFC, NFPA, and the insurer sheet.

On those non-Air-Force Defense facilities the electronic-equipment area is wet pipe throughout. A gaseous system may only supplement. It is out of scope here. Sprinklers below a raised floor are prohibited. Very-early-warning detection with at least two alarm levels is required. Unless the space is constantly occupied by trained staff, power drops on sprinkler waterflow, and the disconnect is downstream of the UPS. A stand-alone area over 2,500 ft² gets a gaseous supplement or sprinklers. Over 7,500 ft², sprinklers are required. Cabling in a plenum or void is plenum-rated or in noncombustible conduit.

The UFC routes telecommunication areas that serve more than one facility to NFPA 76, in a noncombustible building, and carves out the room that only brings service into that one facility. Two rooms that look alike can land on different standards. On a commercial job the NFPA 75 versus NFPA 76 boundary is the adopted code and the owner. The service-footprint question is still the right one to ask (`telecom-rooms.md`).

Lithium-ion battery rooms in the same UFC section are 0.6 gpm/ft² over the entire room, 120 minutes, and at least 500 gpm hose, in 3-hour construction. That is a Defense figure. It is about twice the FM room density in `ess-room-sprinklers.md`. Use it to bracket a Defense job. Do not carry 0.6 onto a commercial FM room as the project number.

The document supersedes NFPA where it speaks, replaces the IFC with NFPA 1, and requires the latest edition of referenced standards. That is the opposite of the commercial rule that the adopted edition is the floor (`edition-pins.md`).

The method, when the contract actually names the UFC, is `most-stringent-basis-matrix`.
