# Private fire service mains

The loop carries the full demand with one leg out of service, at C = 100, on the actual inside diameter. That rule and the friction equation are `campus-loop-and-fire-flow.md`. This note is the pipe, the valves, the hydrants, and the places the sheets disagree. Thrust design is `thrust-restraint.md`.

Keep the fire main separate from domestic and process water. A combined main hides a pressure loss, shares impairment, and can be robbed during a fire. FM DS 3-10 (January 2022 interim) still covers a combined main. The dedicated loop is the campus practice.

## Size and isolate

NFPA 24-2022 and 2025 §5.2 require the hydraulic calculation to show that the main supplies the total demand. A main that supplies a hydrant is at least 6 in. (§5.2.1.2; the 2019 split is §5.2.1 and §5.2.2). Hydraulic calculations are Chapter 11. Underground restraint, thrust blocks, and flushing are Chapter 10. An older map that put sizing in Chapter 13 is not the map to use. An unamended 2024 I-Code adoption typically enforces NFPA 24-2022. The pack's delta baseline remains NFPA 24-2025.

FM DS 3-10 §2.2.10 recommends at least 6 in., velocity under 20 ft/s, and a hydrant lateral of at least 6 in. Smaller than 6 in. only with no hydrants, a calculation, and a main at least as large as the riser. A carrier ladder that starts at 4 in. when a calculation proves it, and steps toward 12 in. above 2,000 gpm, is that carrier. Check friction on the one-leg-out path before you accept the ladder. At 1,500 gpm and C = 100, that carrier's table is about 110 psi per 1,000 ft in 6 in. pipe and about 3.8 psi in 12 in. pipe.

FM §3.1.4.1 sizes loop legs for the no-impairment split, and it pushes total-flow legs mainly where a deluge system is present. Building deluge is out of scope. On wet, dry, and double-interlock halls keep the one-leg-out full demand, and show the FM engineer the delta. Do not silently downsize to the split. A federal criterion that carries 50 percent of the fire flow on a single break is that criterion, and it is less than the one-leg-out rule.

Sectional valves limit an impairment. The carrier wants no more than five fire-protection units between valves. FM DS 3-10 §2.2.11.3 is about six risers per divisional valve. Take the tighter bite on a campus that is built in phases. Put valves on both sides of every supply connection and at the major intersections. A phased stub is a dead end until the next building ties in (`thrust-restraint.md`).

NFPA 24-2022 and 2025 §6.7.2 supervise control valves by a station signal, a constantly attended local signal, locking, or fencing and a weekly inspection. Roadway-box gates are exempt (§6.7.3). Electronic supervision of every control valve 2-1/2 in. and larger is insurer practice, not that exemption. Prefer post indicator valves about 40 ft from a wall. A wall indicator is code-legal and disfavored where collapse is credible. Keep new valves out of pits.

## Hydrants and test water

Dry-barrel hydrants at about 250 ft, out to 300 ft in a remote yard, are a carrier spacing. Local amendments change it. Do not copy another city's 400 ft or 600 ft hose-lay onto the plans. FM DS 3-10 §2.2.8.1 and the carrier agree: no pumper outlet on a private hydrant. A large outlet can pull the sprinkler supply down. Threads match the local fire department. One whole-system fire-department connection, or several that each feed the whole system. Do not provide connections that each feed a fragment. A public hydrant with a pumper outlet within 40 to 50 ft of that connection is the carrier's siting. Confirm the local distance.

Mains other than lead-ins stay at least 5 ft from foundation footings (DS 3-10 §2.2.11.4). Bury at least 6 in. deeper than the municipal main (§2.2.15.1). Confirm frost depth. Do not take it from a figure.

## Material

Ductile iron with polyethylene encasement (AWWA C105/A21.5, film at least 0.008 in. on the FM sheet) is the default unless a soil evaluation clears the wrap. Cement-lined iron is the hydraulic reference. PVC push-on joints are not self-restrained. HDPE butt fusion is restrained and flexible, and the wall grows inward, so the nominal size is not the inside diameter. One comparison of 6 in. DR 11 HDPE with cement-lined ductile iron was about twice the friction, and a heavier wall was about four times. Size HDPE on the specified dimension ratio. Upsize it or run the friction on the real inside diameter (`hydraulic-method.md`). Transition from plastic to metal before the building. The piece through the foundation is often steel, with no joint under the slab.

PVC is not used where aromatic hydrocarbons can spill, and plastic pipe is FM Approved where the FM sheet applies (DS 3-10).

## Test

Hydrostatic test at least 200 psi for 2 hours, or 50 psi above maximum static when static exceeds 150 psi. Include pump churn in that static (DS 3-10 §2.3.1.2). Leakage at most 2 quarts per hour per 100 joints (§2.3.1.6). Do not use the fire pump as the test pump. Flush at 10 ft/s before the connection (NFPA 24 §10.10.2.1 and Table 10.10.2.1.3). The FM table is 880, 1,560, 2,440, and 3,500 gpm for 6, 8, 10, and 12 in. The maintenance sheet's 3,520 gpm at 12 in. is the same 10 ft/s idea (`pump-power-testing.md`). Use the table on the sheet you are following.

Thrust design pressure is the test pressure. The carrier's "50 psi above hydrostatic, minimum 250 psi" is that rule stated as a floor (`thrust-restraint.md`). NFPA 24 §10.6.1.1.1 bases thrust on the test pressure.
