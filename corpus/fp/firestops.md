# Through-penetration firestops

The wall rating that creates the penetration is `compartmentation.md`. A fire damper is not firestopped like a pipe (`dampers.md`). This note is the listed system that closes a pipe, conduit, or cable opening. The obligation to maintain the assembly rating is IBC §714. The materials and the annular gap are the listing. There is no generic sealant that satisfies the section.

Specify the system number, the tested assembly, the penetrant, and the annular-space window. Fill materials are not interchangeable between systems that happen to share an F rating. A submittal that names a product without that system number has not shown compliance. FM DS 5-31 §2.1.1.1 requires an FM Approved firestop equal to the barrier rating, installed by an approved contractor, for cable, tray, and bus penetrations. That still does not pick the system number for a given opening.

Ratings measure different things. F is hours until flame passes. T is hours until the unexposed side rises 325 °F. Many floor penetrations need T. L is airflow per square foot of opening at ambient and at 400 °F, which is the smoke-leakage number for a barrier that must hold a pressure difference (`stair-pressurization.md`, `smoke-purge.md`). W is water resistance, which matters under wet piping and at a slab. Three catalogued systems can share a 3-hour F rating and differ by about fifteen times in ambient L rating, then all tighten once the material intumesces. Specify the L rating on a pressurization boundary. The F rating will look identical either way.

The annular space is a window with a minimum and a maximum. A hole cut larger than the listing fails the same way a hole cut too small fails. Opening area and the maximum single dimension are part of the same listing. Put the opening size on the drawing. Cable tray does not inherit a pipe or duct system. Select a tray system by its own number.

A W rating inside one system number can depend on which of the listed sealants is used. Name the product inside the system.

Every sprinkler or standpipe that crosses a rated barrier is located and detailed on the fire-protection drawings. Language that says only "as required by code" does not specify a system.

The schedule method that flags the barrier is `fire-area-compartmentation-schedule`. The listing is the design of the penetration.
