# Post-earthquake water supply

FM DS 1-11 (July 2016 / interim April 2025) records that an earthquake can impair the public supply and the sprinklers at the same time the fire department is overloaded (§1.1). It does not design the braces or the tank. Section 1.0 sends water-based equipment protection to FM DS 2-8 and tanks to FM DS 3-2. Sprinkler brace spacing is `seismic-bracing.md`. Tank hardware is `water-tanks.md`. Whether to add a second source is `water-supply-reliability.md`.

Use the history as an Appendix C input on a campus in an FM 50- to 500-year zone, not as a gallon demand. Table 3.1.2 rates public water for fire suppression as a complete, widespread loss in several strong events that also produced significant fire, including 1906, 1923, 1994, and 1995. Successful alternate supplies are the exception in that table. A 2010 event in the same section left one city near 10 percent of customers with water immediately and another essentially without water for 3 days, with restoration still incomplete weeks later. Liquefaction of transmission mains is the mechanism to ask about when the campus connection crosses poor soil. Do not plan on the municipal main being back in hours.

## What has to keep working on site

FM DS 2-8 (October 2017 / interim April 2025) §2.2.5 and §2.2.6 are the pump and tank package. The point of the sheet is that the system still works after the earthquake and that a break does not flood the building (§2.1).

- Four-way bracing on vertical discharge risers. Lateral and longitudinal restraint on overhead and pipe-stand piping. Fuel-line flexibility. Anchor the pump, the driver, the controller, the fuel tank, and the batteries.
- Put the jockey on emergency power when that power exists. If a generator feeds the fire-pump motor, the generator package gets the full seismic protection.
- A tank is FM Approved for a zone at least as severe as the site (DS 1-2). An anchored tank gets two flexible couplings: one at the tank wall and one within 2 ft of the pump or the underground entrance. An unanchored tank is detailed for 2 in. of horizontal movement in any direction and 4 in. upward, unless the engineer calculates otherwise.
- Unanchored suction tanks show up in the loss history as a supply impairment, not only as a water-damage loss (§3.2). App D Table D.1 is the turnover checklist for the pump anchorage set, the tank approval, and the pipe flex.

Losses that are mostly water damage from unbraced pipe, weak hangers, and unbraced ceilings stay on the bracing note. This note does not reprint those spacing limits.

Zone accelerations, when site S_DS is unknown, are the FM values already compared in `seismic-bracing.md`. Do not average them with the NFPA Cp. Do not treat other suppression scopes inside DS 2-8 as permission to design them here. Outdoor transformer water spray stays in its own method. A building deluge system stays out of scope.
