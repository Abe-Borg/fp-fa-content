# Freeze exposure and heat trace

Screen freeze from the site's design minimum temperature. Do not invent a 100-year temperature, a cable output in watts per foot, or a controller setpoint. If the number is not given, label it ASSUMED and say why.

Heat trace is not a heated valve house. A double-interlock valve enclosure that must stay above freezing is a heated enclosure. Heat trace is for wet piping that must stay wet and cannot be drained. Dry and double-interlock piping that is drained and supervised with air or nitrogen is not a heat-trace run.

## Listing fence

Check the listing before selecting a cable. A prior retest elected a self-regulating cable and a control panel that sat outside the held listing for fire-protection pipe, then re-solved inside the listing with a different cable and thicker fiberglass insulation. The lesson is the fence, not the lengths, the temperature, or the catalog numbers from that retest. Do not copy them.

Manufacturer tables in the opened sheet stop at a stated start-up temperature (one held sheet stops at −20 °F). Colder air is outside the table. Send it to the manufacturer's calculation. Do not extrapolate.

Alarm the heat-trace controller to the fire-alarm panel as a supervisory signal when the specification calls for it. The circuit design is electrical. The fire-protection note only requires that a failure of the trace is visible to the people who maintain the wet pipe.

Antifreeze systems are not the company default for these halls. Do not substitute an antifreeze loop for a drained dry or double-interlock zone.
