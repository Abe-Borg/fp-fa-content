# Ceilings and concealed spaces

Data-hall raised floors are not this sheet. FM DS 1-12 (April 2020, interim July 2020) §1.0 sends them to FM DS 5-32. See `hall-geometry.md`.

Class 2 concealed materials usually need sprinklers in the space. Class 1 materials usually do not (FM DS 1-12 Table 2.2.2). Where firestops are recommended, subdivide a combustible concealed space at 2,000 ft² or less (§2.2.3.2). Combustible concealed walls get a firestop at each floor and horizontally at 10 ft or less (§2.2.3.3). Do not use composite wood as a firestop. A wood-frame stop is solid wood at least as thick as the member (§2.2.3.4).

Exposed engineered-web joists deeper than 22 in. need a sprinkler in every bay, staggered (§2.3.4).

Melt-out ceilings on this sheet are hazard category 1 and wet pipe only, horizontal, with no insulation above, and no more than 5 ft below the sprinklers. The subsection number was not stated. Confirm it before citing. Cloud ceilings are noncombustible or FM Approved Class 1, and they are obstructions under DS 2-0 (§2.2.2.1.2).

A combustible exterior canopy or eave wider than 30 in., with a combustible exterior wall, is a protection trigger. The subsection was not stated.

Expanded or extruded polystyrene cavity insulation failed the side-by-side tests described on the sheet. Use an FM Approved cavity-wall system, or noncombustible or FM 4880 Class 1 construction (§3.3).

Do not treat the FM DS 1-12 flowchart as the same decision as the NFPA 13-2025 concealed-space omission tables. Read both, and keep the more stringent result that applies. A state amendment can prohibit a drop-out ceiling that NFPA 13 still describes. Check the adopted fire code before specifying drop-out panels. Hall containment drop-out panels, where DS 5-32 allows them, are the Class 4651 product in `hall-geometry.md`, not this melt-out rule.
