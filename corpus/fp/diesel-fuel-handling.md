# Diesel receiving and transfer

Generator-room density and day-tank arrangement are `generator-plant.md`. Drainage and the ceiling density for ignitable liquid are `ignitable-liquid-sprinklers.md`. Outdoor tank spacing on the insurer sheet is FM DS 7-88, April 2020, interim October 2020. This note is the off-load position, the transfer piping, and the emergency fuel shutoff. The geometry below is a 2014 extract of UFC 3-460-01, a Defense petroleum-facility criteria. On a commercial campus it is a comparison. It is not the code floor and it is not the insurer basis. NFPA 30 separation is not restated here. Mark a commercial setback `[V]` until the adopted fire code and the insurer tank sheet are named.

The handling criteria contain no sprinkler density. Foam and water spray are out of scope. The fire-protection items are geometry, valve listing, static control, thermal relief, and a shutoff that can be reached during a fire.

Emergency fuel shutoff is a different device from the information-technology disconnect (`hall-power-isolation.md`) and from a radio-system disconnect (`errcs.md`). It stops fueling at the off-load area and the associated pump pad. Place it 100 ft to 200 ft from the pump or the position, on the route people use to leave, with maintained contacts so it latches. Provide one per position and one at each delivery or receipt point. Label it so it is not mistaken for a room disconnect.

The same extract puts a truck or rail receiving position 50 ft from buildings, roads, overhead power lines, pad-mounted transformers, and property lines, and 25 ft from the fuel-farm fence. A loading position is 100 ft from a railroad track. Those distances stack with transformer containment spacing in `transformer-siting.md`, which is measured from the inside edge of containment. Draw both on the site plan. They answer different documents.

Containment at the position is concrete, not asphalt, pitched at least 1 percent to the drains. The drain valve is normally closed, lockable, with an indicator post, outside the containment, and reachable during a fire. Drain piping in this extract excludes clay, concrete, fiberglass, and plastic. FM DS 7-83 (January 2012, interim January 2015) prefers no valve on drainage piping, or a locked-open valve if one exists (§2.2.3.8). That conflicts with a normally closed containment drain. Treat the closed valve as the containment drain, outside the continuous drainage path, or ask the insurer. Trench sizing is `ignitable-liquid-sprinklers.md`.

Valves in fuel service are fire-safe to API 607. Ball valves to API 608. Checks to API 6D. Aluminum, cast iron, and bronze bodies are excluded by the extract. Above-ground tanks over 12,000 gal take a double block and bleed on shell connections in that extract. A fusible-link butterfly at 165 °F is described as the first device in the direction of flow at a loading position. Some Defense projects prohibit it. Confirm before specifying it.

Any segment that can be isolated at both ends gets thermal relief. Static relaxation of about 30 seconds applies where conductivity is at most 50 pS/m and no dissipater additive is used. Ground verification blocks a dedicated pump start, or alarms when a shared valve opens.

Outdoor tanks themselves are DS 7-88 Table 1 for tank-to-building and tank-to-tank distances, not this extract. Hydrant reach on that sheet is at most 200 ft, from two directions, outside the dike. A listed double-wall tank does not replace emergency drainage (`generator-plant.md`).

The pump and supply method for fire water is `hydrant-supply-curve`. Fuel-pump redundancy in the extract is full capacity with the largest pump out of service. That is a fuel-system rule. It is the opposite of how a fire pump is started (`fire-pump-installation.md`).
