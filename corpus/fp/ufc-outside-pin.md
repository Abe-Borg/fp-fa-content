# Federal criteria are not the I-Code pin

UFC 3-600-01 is a Department of Defense instrument. Do not apply its density table, its C-factor permission, or its performance-based rules as the unamended IBC and IFC pin on a commercial hall. When a project is actually under that document, design to the edition named in the contract and say so. The notes below are the collisions, not a second basis of design.

The held water-based text is dated 8 August 2016 and is also labeled Change 6 in later citations. Discharge for non-storage occupancies comes from that document's table by hazard, ceiling height, and a wet or dry column, not from NFPA 13 occupancy densities. Dry pipe and double-interlock use the dry column. Single-interlock uses the wet column and remains comparison-only.

Ordinary hazard at 30 ft or less on that table matches the published hall pair of 0.2 gpm/ft² over 2,500 ft² wet or 3,500 ft² dry. Do not import the rest of the table because that one row matches. Extra hazard, heights above 30 ft, and the hose step above 60 ft are different numbers. A ceiling slope steeper than 2 in 12 adds 30 percent to the design area, and the quick-response area reduction is not permitted. Pressure-reducing valves are banned on dedicated fire piping. Hydraulic calculations use at least 12 psi for a reduced-pressure backflow assembly and 8 psi for a double check. Nitrogen to at least 98 percent within 14 days, with permission to calculate at C = 120, is that document. Do not apply C = 120 on an I-Code calculation (`nitrogen-compressor-and-c-factor.md`).

Pipe schedule in that document is Schedule 40 through 2 in. and Schedule 10 above 2 in. A commercial hall on FM minimum wall can differ (`pipe-layout.md`).

Do not use a derived density table that claims to be adapted from the document. One such table printed a light-hazard density that is not on the wet and dry columns.

Performance-based design in the Change 6 appendix is a federal process: at least two trial designs, no credit for a method that only looks complete when a scenario is omitted, no occupant load below the prescriptive densities, and an annual warrant that tracks changes. Full prescriptive compliance meets the goals without scenario analysis. On commercial work the authority, the owner, and the insurer each accept or refuse a departure on their own standard. The federal appendix does not grant that departure.

A federal wet-pipe specification that sprinklers electrical rooms regardless of the NFPA 13 omission is that specification. It is not a general electrical-room rule.
