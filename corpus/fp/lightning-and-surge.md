# Lightning and surge on campus electrical gear

Room sprinklers for switchgear are `mv-switchgear-room.md`. Transformer spacing and spray are `transformer-siting.md`. This note is direct-stroke shielding and surge arresters for exposed electrical distribution. The sheet is FM DS 5-11, April 2012, interim October 2025. Building lightning protection for an ordinary structure is NFPA 780 and UL 96A. This sheet points at NFPA 780 for ignitable-liquid and flammable-gas buildings (§2.1.2.8).

More than 40 thunderstorm days a year is severe exposure. Shield-wire protective angle is then 20 degrees. Moderate exposure uses 30 to 45 degrees. Shield the substation and at least a half mile of supply line, and ground the shield wire at every pole (§2.1.1.1).

Keep protected objects under the rolling sphere in Appendix C.4. That appendix uses a 150 ft radius. Section 2.1.1.2 says a 150 ft diameter. Treat the conflict as unresolved until the figures are read on the project, and prefer the appendix geometry at design.

Arresters sit close to the equipment terminals. The ground electrode is at most 1 ohm. Leads are short, and the arrester ground bonds to the equipment frame so the insulation sees the arrester drop (§2.1.2.2). Measure earth resistance annually where the water table moves, otherwise every 5 years. Inspect connections annually.

Protective margin is greater than 20 percent against basic impulse insulation level and chopped-wave withstand (Appendix C.8). Arrester rating is at least 1.25 times maximum line-to-ground voltage. Ungrounded systems use 100 percent of system voltage. Class follows size: station class at or above 7.5 MVA, intermediate from 1 to 20 MVA, distribution for 15 kV dry-type and small machines.

Effectively shielded lines are checked near 5 kA. A typical line at or below 34.5 kV that is not effectively shielded is checked at 20 kA, with arresters at the transformer terminals (Appendix C.8.4). Separation of 75 ft to 200 ft can be acceptable at or above 23 kV with full insulation level. At or below 15 kV the separation tolerance is zero.

A 15 kV dry-type transformer often has a 60 kV basic impulse level against 95 kV for an oil-filled distribution unit. Overhead or long-secondary-cable feeds get low-sparkover distribution arresters at the dry-type terminals (Appendix C.8.4).

Metal-clad switchgear fed from exposed overhead lines uses station-type arresters where the connection is direct or the metallic sheath is not continuous (§2.1.2.7.1). Continuous metallic-sheath cable gets arresters at the junction, and may still need them at the switchgear (§2.1.2.7.2).

Tables and examples in the appendix are older ANSI coordination methods. Use the recommendation text as the insurer bar. Re-pin discharge voltages to the current IEEE C62 data and the manufacturer before construction. Gapless metal-oxide arresters do not use the gapped-valve worked example.

The room method remains `mv-switchgear-room-package`. Surge is additional, not a substitute.
