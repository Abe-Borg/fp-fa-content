# Heat-trace listing and supervision

Heat trace is the wet-pipe option where the pipe must stay wet and the space cannot be held above freezing. Drained dry and double-interlock pipe is not a heat-trace run. The listing fence, and the refusal to invent watts per foot, is `freeze-heat-trace.md`. The campus temperature program is `cold-climate-freeze.md`.

## What the sprinkler standard requires

On NFPA 13-2019, 2022, and 2025 the branch-line rules keep the same numbers:

- The cable is specifically listed for branch lines (§16.4.1.4.1).
- Electrical supervision positively confirms that the circuit is energized (§16.4.1.4.2). The section does not choose supervisory versus trouble. Agree that class with the authority and the owner, and land it on the fire-alarm panel.
- Heat tracing does not replace a heated valve enclosure (§8.1.4). NFPA 13-2025 repeats that bar for dry systems at §8.2.3.2.3 and for preaction at §8.3.1.7.2.3.

Do not heat-trace a dry or double-interlock valve or its trim. One listed dry-valve instruction forbids it because mineral deposits form on the trim. Hold the valve and trim at or above 40 °F in a heated enclosure.

Insulation is not automatically an obstruction. If it sits at or below the deflector and changes the discharge, apply the obstruction rules. A manufacturer listing that wants upright heads insulated to the reducer on a 45 degree taper is that listing. Judge the spray shadow from the insulation outside diameter.

A 155 °F minimum sprinkler temperature appears on manufacturer listings. It is not an NFPA 13 sentence. Coordinate it with `sprinkler-temperature-and-k.md`.

## Power

Article 695 does not cover heat trace. Ground-fault protection of equipment is required on the heating cable (NEC 427.22). The industrial exception needs a ground-fault alarm, qualified maintenance, and a reason the circuit must stay in service.

Optional standby (Article 702) is the default power class until a cited code or the authority requires legally required standby or emergency. If the building has backup power, put the trace on it and include the kilowatts in the generator and transfer-switch load. Confirm the adopted NEC edition before citing the article number.

## Listing, not a catalog

The listed unit is the cable and the controller together, under the fire-sprinkler heat-trace category. A cable that is listed only with a general-purpose controller is not a branch-line system. Plastic sprinkler pipe stays outside the listing unless that exact pair says otherwise.

Do not copy watt-per-foot, circuit length, or setpoint tables from one manufacturer onto another. One controller's printed maintain point can sit in Celsius and will not match the 40 °F water floor. Use the guide that owns the cable.

Buried supply on a listed system keeps splices and tees above grade. A 2 ft cover in one guide is that guide's minimum. Frost depth for the fire main is still `private-fire-mains.md`.

There is no heat-trace chapter to import from NFPA 25 in this pack. Supervise the circuit, and alarm a failure to the people who maintain the wet pipe.
