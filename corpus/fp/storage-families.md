# ESFR, CMSA, and CMDA limits

ESFR sprinklers are used only in wet pipe systems unless the head is specifically listed for dry or preaction (NFPA 13-2022 and 2025 §14.2.2). Double-interlock storage does not default to ESFR.

CMSA is permitted in wet, dry, or preaction systems when it is installed to its listing (NFPA 13-2022 §13.2.2; NFPA 13-2025 §13.2.1.3).

ESFR spacing is not more than 12 ft when storage is 25 ft or less and the ceiling is 30 ft or less, and not more than 10 ft if either limit is exceeded (NFPA 13-2025 §14.2.8.1). Temperature rating is ordinary or intermediate, with no 25 ft trigger for intermediate (NFPA 13-2025 §14.2.5).

Coverage figures of 64 ft² to 100 ft², a 36 in. deflector clearance, and a 2 ft draft curtain where ESFR meets standard-response heads come from a secondary summary. Confirm them in NFPA 13-2025 before sealing. Ceiling-only ESFR over solid-shelf racks or open-top containers is the same status: confirm the section. Do not seal a number this note marks as unconfirmed.

CMSA minimum K-factor of 11.2 and coverage of 80 ft² to 130 ft² are the same kind of summary. Confirm the section. CMDA area is capped at 100 ft² where the density is at least 0.25 gpm/ft², again confirm before sealing.

For CMDA storage, dry pipe and every preaction type take a 30 percent area increase with density unchanged (NFPA 13-2025 §20.16.3). That section is broader than the ordinary-hazard increase in §19.2.3.2.6. Do not also apply an FM absolute area. See `density-area.md`.

CMDA ordinary-temperature densities apply to ordinary and intermediate sprinklers. High-temperature densities apply to high-temperature sprinklers (NFPA 13-2025 §21.1.6). A vendor claim that the density inverts at 25 ft is not that sentence.

Rack storage above 25 ft on CMDA requiring in-rack sprinklers, a 40,000 ft² limit on one in-rack system, and a separate valve when more than 20 in-rack sprinklers are present, are a 2010 vendor reading. Confirm both in NFPA 13-2025 before sealing. A 40 ft storage and 45 ft ceiling ban on CMSA and ESFR is unverified against current listings. Mark it `[V]`.

K-14 permission above 35 ft was removed in NFPA 13-2016 and later. The cited replacement is K16.8. Confirm that in NFPA 13-2025 before using it.

Quick response means a response-time index of 50 (m·s)^1/2 or less and a quick-response listing. Standard response means 80 (m·s)^1/2 or more (NFPA 13-2025 §3.3.223).

High-piled combustible storage in the IFC starts when the top of storage exceeds 12 ft, or 6 ft for high-hazard commodities (Chapter 32; confirm IFC 2024). High-piled areas add together for the permit table unless separated by a 1-hour barrier or, where the building is sprinklered, by at least 100 ft clear of high-piled storage. Confirm the adopted table. Where the adopted fire code and NFPA 13 conflict, the fire code governs. FM DS 8-9 can apply above 200 ft² at any height, which is earlier than the 12 ft trigger. Use the more stringent.

In-rack flow is added at the point of connection the same way as in `storage-design.md`.
