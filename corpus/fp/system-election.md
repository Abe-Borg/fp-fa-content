# Wet, dry, and double-interlock election

The company basis is wet pipe, dry pipe, or double-interlock preaction. Foam, clean agent, water mist, deluge, and water spray are out of scope. Record the request and stop. Single-interlock and non-interlock are comparison-only. If either is offered as the basis of design, record it and return the question. Do not substitute double-interlock in silence, and do not substitute the other way.

FM DS 5-32 (January 2026) §2.4.1.1 ranks water-based protection in this order: wet, then non-interlock, then single-interlock, then double-interlock last. Double-interlock remains an allowed dry design on that sheet. Company practice keeps double-interlock on an FM hall and flags the rank to the owner and the insurer on every such job.

Inside a data-processing room the wet demand area is 2,500 ft² and the double-interlock area is 3,500 ft², both at 0.20 gpm/ft² plus 250 gpm hose (`density-area.md`). Electing double-interlock moves the demand from 750 gpm and 45,000 gallons to 950 gpm and 57,000 gallons at 60 minutes. Non-interlock and single-interlock, if they were the basis, would stay on the wet area inside this occupancy (§2.4.3.2.1.2). They are not the basis.

Outside a DS 5-32 occupancy, FM DS 2-0 (October 2021 / interim January 2024) §2.2.1.12 defaults preaction to a dry design. The wet ceiling basis on that section is a single-interlock comparison, including smoke detectors at most 15 ft apart on a smooth ceiling. Do not use it to size a double-interlock hall.

A temperate space, about 40 °F to 200 °F, is where DS 2-0 §2.2.4.2.1 discusses single-interlock. A space that stays below 20 °F with storage is where §2.2.5.1.2 discusses double-interlock. Freeze protection for a wet system is `freeze-heat-trace.md`. Do not swap families to dodge a freeze.

One carrier white paper (March 2026) prefers single-interlock on an Ordinary Hazard Group 1 hall and states no density, no area, and no K-factor. It treats double-interlock as a 30 percent area increase plus a nitrogen fill. That is a different layer from FM DS 5-32. Report the delta. Do not let the white paper erase the FM absolute area, and do not let FM erase a stricter owner number. The most stringent applicable layer governs (`../design-conventions.md`).

An owner standard can raise protection. It cannot lower the adopted code floor or an insurer condition of coverage.
