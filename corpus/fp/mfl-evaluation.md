# Maximum foreseeable loss, as a scenario

Numeric wall ratings and separation distances are `mfl-walls.md`. Ordinary exposure, with sprinklers credited, is `campus-exterior-exposure.md`. An interior hall-to-hall fire barrier is `compartmentation.md`. This note is what the loss scenario means. The sheet is FM DS 1-22, April 2019, interim January 2026.

The scenario assumes active sprinklers, detection, and safety devices are impaired. The only active exception the definition credits is an FM Approved and tested maximum-foreseeable-loss fire door (§2.1 and Appendix A). Everything else that stops the event is a physical limiting factor sized on DS 1-42. Natural hazards are excluded from this scenario (§1.1).

Evaluate four parts before drawing a wall: the peril, the timeline with actives out, the property damage, and the business interruption, including verified make-up capacity (§3.1). A wall that does not change the interruption story is the wrong tool. Make-up counts only when the continuity plan has been tested (§3.1.4.3).

Campus layout is the first control. Put redundant capacity in separate buildings outside the same loss footprint (§2.3.1.C). Keep generator yards, fuel, and outdoor transformers from owning the hall loss by space or by the exposure package. Filling a historic fire-break yard with a new hall erases the credit (§3.3.4).

Three walls get confused on the same plan:

- An IBC fire barrier between halls is `compartmentation.md`.
- An IBC fire wall that creates separate buildings is the same note, under §706.
- An FM loss-limiting wall assumes the sprinklers are impaired. Hours and openings are `mfl-walls.md`.

A 2-hour interior fire barrier is not that third wall. Glossary items that live in this sheet: a noncombustible wall endures 27 kW/m² without ignition or joint failure (Appendix A), and a high-rise occupied floor is more than 75 ft above the lowest fire-department vehicle access.

Limiting factors need inspection the same way any credited barrier does (§2.5.2). A new penetration or a rack line against the wall is a management-of-change item.

Explosion limitation by damage-limiting construction is one tool the sheet names (§3.3.4). The envelope numbers are `damage-limiting-construction.md`.

The interior schedule method is `fire-area-compartmentation-schedule`. It does not size this scenario. Use it for the sprinklered hall barrier, then decide separately whether the campus also needs a loss-limiting wall.
