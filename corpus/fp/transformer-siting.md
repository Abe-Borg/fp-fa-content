# Transformer rooms and outdoor siting

Apparatus water-spray demand for an outdoor oil-filled transformer is `power-block-transformer-water-spray`. This note is whether the unit may be indoors, what the room is, and how far the containment sits from a wall or the next unit. The sheet is FM DS 5-4, July 2019, interim October 2020. It governs the transformer. It does not say "data center." Campus exposure of a building face that is not this apparatus is `campus-exterior-exposure.md`.

The fire-protection half of the sheet applies to all transformers. The electrical testing half applies to large critical units. Distribution units at or below 5 MVA route electrical maintenance to DS 5-20. They still use this sheet for fire protection (§1.0).

Classify the hardware before measuring. An FM Approved transformer under Approval Standard 3990, naturally cooled, in the listing's kilovolt-ampere range, sits 3 ft from a wall of any type and needs no fire protection (Table 5). An approved fluid in a transformer that is not itself approved is a different row, 5 ft to 50 ft depending on volume and wall. A non-approved fluid runs 5 ft to 100 ft. The glossary is explicit that an approved fluid does not make the transformer approved. Tables 5, 6, and 8 measure from the inside edge of containment, not from the tank.

Dry-type and gas-insulated units indoors need a noncombustible room and no liquid protection (Table 4, §2.2.2.1). That is the usual indoor campus case. Every FM-acceptable indoor location has direct access from outdoors (Fig. 2 and §2.2.1.2). A detached building may be judged as an indoor location or as an outdoor transformer (Table 4, footnote a). Pick the regime the site can meet.

Indoor units stay at least 3 ft from walls (§2.2.1.6). A non-approved fluid above 100 gal in one room pushes a 3-hour room, and a 3-hour subdivision per transformer when several share it, unless the protection in §2.2.3 is provided. Current indoor sprinkler language for an approved fluid is 0.2 gpm/ft² over the entire room. An older 0.3 gpm/ft² indoor figure is the 2005 appendix, not the current indoor row. The outdoor apparatus spray density is a different number and is sized in the skill above. Gaseous flooding of an indoor transformer room is out of scope. The sheet removed that option.

Outdoor containment holds at least 60 minutes of 500 gpm hose plus the simultaneous spray, and extends at least 5 ft beyond fluid-containing parts up to 1,000 gal, or 8 ft above that, including external coolers. Indoor containment for a special system holds the liquid volume plus 20 minutes of system flow, with a curb of at least 3 in, one basin per transformer. Environmental rules can ask for more. The sheet does not claim to meet them (§2.3.1.4).

When wall protection uses a 3-hour barrier or water spray, Table 7 asks whether the roof is radiantly exposed. A building at least 50 ft tall is not exposed at any volume. A single-story hall is usually under that height. An exposed roof band gets a Class A cover. Distances in Tables 5, 6, and 8 are fire spacing. Section 2.3.1 says they may not protect surroundings from rupture overpressure.

Smoke exhaust for an indoor room, where provided, is fed from a source the pre-fire plan does not de-energize (§2.2.2.3). Water-spray hardware outdoors is at least 18 in off the transformer (§2.3.2.1). Weatherproofing of indoor spray, where the skill sizes spray, includes a curb and baffles or a weatherproof design (FM DS 4-1N, May 2010).

The method is `power-block-transformer-water-spray`.
