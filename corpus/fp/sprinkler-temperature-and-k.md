# Sprinkler temperature rating and K-factor

On an FM hall, FM DS 5-32 (January 2026) §2.4.3.3.1.1(B) sets nominal 135 °F on wet pipe and nominal 165 °F on a designed-dry system. Ambient limits that follow from the 50 °F damage rule are in `hall-geometry.md`.

NFPA 13-2025 Table 7.2.4.1(a): a 135 °F glass bulb is limited to 100 °F ceiling ambient, a 155 °F bulb to 120 °F, and 175 °F or 200 °F bulbs to 150 °F. The FM Approval Guide ordinary range, 135 °F to 170 °F, is limited to 100 °F ambient. Intermediate, 175 °F to 225 °F, is limited to 150 °F. Glass-bulb sprinklers are not subject to solder cold flow below their rating (FM DS 2-81 §3.1.4). Use a glass bulb where the ceiling can run warm.

New construction does not use 1/2 in. sprinklers with a K-factor above 5.6 (NFPA 13-2025 §9.4.6; NFPA 13-2022 §9.4.5). A new K8.0 head is 3/4 in.

NFPA 13-2025 Table 7.2.2.1: K5.6 is 5.3 to 5.8 on 1/2 in.; K8.0 is 7.4 to 8.2 on 3/4 in. or 1/2 in.; K11.2 is 10.7 to 11.7 on 1/2 in. or 3/4 in. The nominal orifice in Table A.7.2.2.1 is 1/2 in. at K5.6, 17/32 in. at K8.0, and 5/8 in. at K11.2. No nominal orifice is listed at K16.8 and above. Calculate flow from the listed K. Dry sprinklers use the adjusted K (NFPA 13-2025 §28.2.4.10.3).

The minimum operating pressure for these UL and FM heads under NFPA 13 is 7 psi (NFPA 13-2022 and 2025 §28.2.4.11.1). A 5 psi minimum printed for another listing body does not apply. At 0.2 gpm/ft² the FM floor is K5.6 standard coverage or K11.2 extended coverage (FM DS 2-0 Table 2.5.2.1.1(b)).

Treat FM working pressure as 175 psi unless the approval chart for that exact head, finish, and escutcheon shows a higher FM rating. A 250 psi mark that is only a UL or cUL rating is not an FM rating. Changing the escutcheon can drop the FM approval even when the K-factor is unchanged. Read the approval chart for the escutcheon that will be installed. Do not freeze a model number from another hall.

Start pressure at a head is the larger of the listed minimum and the pressure from the flow and the K-factor. At 26 gpm that is about 21.6 psi on K5.6 and about 10.6 psi on K8.0. Those two figures illustrate the equation. They are not a remote-area design.
