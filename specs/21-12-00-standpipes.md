# Section 21 12 00 — Fire-suppression standpipes (draft)

Draft language. Verify with the engineer of record before issue. This section elects a Class I standpipe and states residual and supervision. It does not size the hall sprinkler demand and it does not replace the pump envelope.

## Part 1 — General

### 1.1 Summary

Provide Class I standpipes where the adopted fire code, the owner standard, or the building height requires them. A single-story hall often does not trip a height trigger by itself. Do not treat one locality's administrative rule as the model code. Hose may be omitted only where the adopted amendment says so and the fire code official approves it in writing. Valves and connections remain.

### 1.2 Residual and demand

Carry 100 psi residual at the most remote 2-1/2 in. outlet (NFPA 14-2024 §10.2.6.1 and §10.2.6.1.2). The 65 psi figure applies to 1-1/2 in. outlets only. Keep any lower 2-1/2 in. residual as comparison-only. Ask the authority having jurisdiction in writing before using it. Do not merge the two figures.

Demand in gallons per minute follows the enforced NFPA 14 edition. If the adopted code's referenced-standards chapter has no NFPA 14 row, the edition is unverified. Say so. Do not invent an edition year.

Coordinate pressure zones with Section 21 30 00. Do not schedule a pump from the standpipe residual alone.

## Part 2 — Products

### 2.1 Hose valves and supervisory air

Specify hose valves, caps, and gauges by size and listing. Do not elect a catalog number.

Where the standpipe is manual-dry, NFPA 14-2024 requires supervisory air: 7 psi minimum unless the manufacturer permits lower, and 20 psi maximum (§8.3.2.1.1 through §8.3.2.1.3). NFPA 14-2019 requires monitoring and sets no psi band, so a 7 to 20 psi band is not a 2019 requirement. Name the edition.

Size the air compressor to the dry standpipe volume. Do not freeze a compressor model number. Do not reuse the sprinkler nitrogen generator from Section 21 13 16 or Section 21 13 19.

## Part 3 — Execution

### 3.1 Acceptance

Acceptance testing follows the enforced NFPA 14. A hydrostatic test held in the pack for that standard is at least 200 psi for 2 hours, or 50 psi above working pressure, whichever is greater. Confirm the section against the edition in force before issuing the number as a citation.

Provide a drain path that can be used without taking the sprinkler riser out of service when the drawings show a combined system. Alarm and valve supervision are specified in Section 28 31 00.
